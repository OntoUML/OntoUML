Events
======

An **event** is a concrete individual that unfolds in time by accumulating temporal parts. At any instant during which an event occurs, only a part of it is present, and the event as a whole exists once all of its parts have occurred. The Second World War, the FIFA 2002 World Cup final, the signing of a contract, and a birth are events. The philosophical literature also calls events perdurants or occurrents.

Events are manifestations of dispositions, a kind of aspect, inhering in endurants. The flow of current in a conductor manifests the conductivity of that conductor. A jog manifests the jogger's intention and capacities. An event therefore existentially depends on the endurants that participate in it.

Events can have parts. A soccer match is composed of two halves. A testing process consists of testing activities. The part of an event that depends exclusively on one endurant is that endurant's **participation**: Brutus' stabbing of Caesar comprises the participations of Brutus, of Caesar, and of the dagger.

Events can be instantaneous or extended. Caesar's death occurs at a single instant. The Second World War extended over six years. From the way events are located in time, temporal relations are derived: one event precedes another, overlaps it, or occurs during it.

Events cannot genuinely change, because they are past entities: they exist after they have occurred. An event has all of its parts, participants, and properties necessarily. An apparent change is either a variation across temporal parts, as when a jog starts at walking pace and ends at running pace, or a change in the endurant of which the event is a manifestation.

Because events are past, a model that includes them is read with a historical, growing-block view of time. The relevant past continues to exist as new events and their participants are added. A reference to a future event is a reference to a belief that an event of some type will happen, or to a commitment to bring an event of some type about, rather than to an event that already exists.
